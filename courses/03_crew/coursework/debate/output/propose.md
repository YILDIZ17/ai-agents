I strongly support the motion: **There should be strict laws to regulate LLMs**.

LLMs are not just harmless tools; they are powerful systems that can shape public opinion, spread misinformation, automate deception, and be used for fraud, harassment, and manipulation at massive scale. Unlike ordinary software, their outputs can sound authoritative even when they are wrong. That makes them especially dangerous in areas like politics, education, healthcare, finance, and security.

Without strict regulation, companies have too much incentive to rush deployment, cut safety corners, and prioritize profit over public harm. Voluntary guidelines are not enough because they are unenforceable. We need clear legal standards for transparency, data use, safety testing, liability, and accountability. If an LLM causes measurable harm, there must be legal responsibility.

Strict laws would not stop innovation. They would make innovation safer and more trustworthy. We regulate medicines, cars, food, and planes because when a product can seriously harm people, society has a duty to set rules. LLMs now belong in that category. The question is not whether regulation limits them — it is whether we allow them to grow without control until the damage becomes irreversible.

For these reasons, strict laws to regulate LLMs are not optional; they are necessary.
